Western blotting

Materials/Equipment

  • Centrifuge cooled to 4℃
  • TissueLyser LT (Qiagen)
  • Sonicator (Branson digital sonifier)
  • BioRad Mini Protein Gel system

Reagents

  • RIPA Lysis buffer

  • Store at 4 ℃
  • Supplement with protease inhibitor and phosphatase inhibitor before use.
    • Protease Inhibitor (100x, Thermo Scientific #1862209)
    • Protease and Phosphatase Inhibitor (if analyzing phosphorylated proteins, 100x, Thermo Scientific #1862495)

Procedures

  1. Protein lysate prep

    • Lysis buffer volume

      Tissue/cell Vol (μL)
      60 mm dish 100-200
      100 mm dish 300-500
      Kidney tissue 15-30 mg 150-300
      1. Use about 1/10 of a whole mouse kidney.
      2. Rule of thumb: 10 μL of lysis buffer per 1mg of tissue.
      3. Thaw standards on ice before preparing lysates.
    • For cell culture: scrape cells off the plate in lysis buffer
    • For kidney tissue: use TissueLyser LT
      1. Place 2 ml microcentrifuge tubes containing 1 stainless steel bead (5 mm mean diameter) on dry ice for at least 15 min.
      2. Transfer tissue piece to the tube and incubate on dry ice for 15 min
      3. Put the tube into the TissueLyser Adapter, incubate for 2 min at RT.
      4. Add lysis buffer (with inhibitors added) to the tube.
      5. Operate at 50Hz for 1 min (Add additional 1 min cycle as needed, let the tube rest on ice between cycles)
      6. Centrifuge the tubes (300g 5min) and transfer the content into a new ep tube.
    • Sonicate the samples briefly at 10% amplitude for 5 sec
    • Centrifuge the lysate at 14,000 RPM (~ 18,000 RCF) for 10 min at 4 ℃

    • Collect supernatant.

      1. Measure protein concentration now. It makes it easier to aliquot/store the lysates and run gel.
      2. Detergent in RIPA interferes with BradFord, dilute the sample at least 10x for measurement.
      3. Bradford Assay is generally performed with 96-well plate (transparent ELISA plates for measuring absorbance)
      4. Prepare standard with BSA (dissolve in lysis buffer) ranging from 2 mg/mL to 0.25 mg/mL in 1:10 diluted RIPA.
      5. Use 18 μL H2O, 2 μL of lysate/standard plus 180 μL 1x bradford solution.
      6. Measure absorbance at 600 nm.
      7. Optional but recommended: bring the samples to similar concentrations for more accurate/equal loading.

    • Aliquot and store lyste at -20 or -80 ℃.

      Keep lysis buffer and lysate cold at all times

  2. SDS-PAGE and Sample Preparation
    • Prepare resolving gel of suitable percentage (see recipe)
    • layer on top with about 1ml of EtOH to make the interface between resolving and stacking gel form a sharp line when it polymerizes (takes about 20-30 min).
    • Pour out the ethanol and rinse with milli-Q water. Dry with paper towel. Take care not to disrupt the interface.

    • Pour the stacking gel (see recipe) and let it sit at least 30 min before taking the comb out.

      • If protein amount is not known, Bradford Assay can be done when the gel is polymerizing.
      • If protein is frozen, thaw on ice when preparing gel
      • Standard loading vol for 1.5mm 10-well gel is 20 µL
      • Boil lysate for 5min after adding DTT (1M stock) and sample buffer
      • During this time, set up the gel using 1x running buffer
    • Load 8-10 ul of protein ladder.

    • After samples are loaded, run the gel first at 80 V. After samples reach resolving (bottom) gel part, turn it up to 120 V until done.

      • Make sure that the loading dye runs out at the bottom
      • Run gel
  3. Semi-Dry Transfer
    • During the gel run, prepare the transfer buffer.
    • Cut a PVDF membrane out (9.5 cm by 5cm) and notch the right hand corner and write on the right hand corner in pencil (helps remind what side the protein is on). Written side of the membrane will face the SDS-gel.

    • Place membrane in methanol for 5 min. Balance the membrane in 1x transfer buffer.

      • PVDF membrane is hydrophobic. Make sure to soak in methanol first.
    • soak two (for each gel) thick filter paper in transfer buffer.
    • Take gel out of apparatus and cut off the stacking. Cut a notch on the top left-hand corner.
    • Balance the gel in 1x transfer buffer for 5-10 min. Careful not to flip the gel.
    • Assemble the transfer sandwich in the transfer instrument, in the order below (bottom to top).
      1. Thick filter paper
      2. PVDF (writing facing up, cut and writing on the top right corner)
      3. Gel (flip up side down, the cut corner on top right, align with the cut on the membrane)
      4. Thick filter paper
    • Roll out bubbles.
    • Cover the lid for the instrument. Transfer at 15v for 30 min. Time can be extended for larger protein.
  4. Blocking and antibody incubations
    • Following transfer, wash the membrane in 1x TBS for 5 to 10 minutes with gentle agitation at room temperature twice.
    • Place membrane carefully in blocking buffer. Gently agitate the solution and incubate for 1 hour at RT.

    • Incubate with primary antibody (see Antibody dilution information) overnight at 4 °C.

      • 6 mL of diluted antibody is needed if plastic pouch is used.
    • Next morning, wash the membrane four times with TBST for 5 min
    • Second antibody incubation – Prepare secondary antibody solution as follows:

    • Odyssey secondaries can be used for detecting two (or more) proteins on the same blot

      Cat# Host Target Conjugation Dilution
      926-32210 Goat Mouse IRDye® 800CW 1:10,000
      926-68070 Goat Mouse IRDye® 680RD 1:5,000
      926-32211 Goat Rabbit IRDye® 800CW 1:10,000
      926-68071 Goat Rabbit IRDye® 680RD 1:5,000
      • It is generally recommended that the IRDye 800CW secondary antibody (800 nm channel) be used to detect the lower-abundance protein target and IRDye 680RD secondary antibody (700nm) to detect the more abundant protein

    • Alternatively, HRP conjugated secondaries are available for chemiluminescence detection.

      Name Company Cat# Host Dilution
      Anti-Mouse jacksonimmuno 115-035-003 Goat 1:10000
      Anti-rabbit Cell Signaling #7074 Goat 1:2000
      Anti-Goat Novex A16005 Donkey 1:5000

      • Incubate at room temperature for 1 hour with agitation. Protect from light
      • Washes
        1. Wash four times with TBST for 5 min
        2. Rinse the membrane with TBS briefly to remove residual Tween 20.
  5. Imaging with LI-COR Odyssey XF system.
    • Start with 10-20 min
    • Image at 700nm even with HRP-conjugated secondaries to better visualized ladder

Recipes for gels and buffers

  1. Polyacrylamide gel

    Reagents Stacking Resolving 7.5% Resolving 10% Resolving 12%
    30% Acrylamide/bis (mL) 1.3 5 6.6 8
    0.5M Tris-HCl, pH 6.8 (mL) 2.5 0 0 0
    1.5M Tris-HCl, pH 8.8 (mL) 0 5 5 5
    10% SDS (uL) 100 200 200 200
    H2O (mL) 6.1 9.8 8.2 6.8
    TEMED (uL) 10 20 20 20
    10% APS (uL) 100 64 64 64

    This calculation is enough for 2 gels with 1.5mm spacer. 10% APS is aliquoted in 200 uL and kept at -20 °C for 6-9 months.

  2. 10x running buffer

    Reagents Concentration Weight Weight
    glycine 192 mM 72.2g 144.4g
    Tris 25 mM 15.15g 30.3
    SDS 1% 5g 10g
    H2O   500 mL 1000 mL

    pH of the 10x running buffer should be 8.3 and no pH adjustment is required. Store the running buffer at room temperature and dilute to 1X before use.

  3. 10x transfer buffer (Bjerrum Schafer-Nielsen)

    Reagents Concentration Weight
    glycine 39 mM 29.3g
    Tris 48 mM 58.2g
    H2O   1000 mL

    pH for transfer buffer should be ~9.2. Do NOT add acid or base to adjust pH. For 1x buffer, add 100mL of 10x stock, 200 mL of Methanol to 700 mL of H2O

  4. 10x TBS

    Reagents Weight
    Tris-HCl 24g
    Tris 5.6g
    NaCl 88g
    H2O to 1L

    PH should be around 7.6 (no need to adjust pH). Add Tween-20 to 0.2% in 1x TBS for TBST

  5. Blocking buffer 5% BSA in TBS
  6. Antibody buffer Dilute blocking buffer 1:10 in TBST
